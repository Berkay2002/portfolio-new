# Photos r3: film strip and dealt prints in their own section

Round 2 (`photos-r2.md`) moved the photos out of About into their own section, "06 / OFF THE CLOCK",
between About and Contact. Berkay liked that and wants to see both the film strip (r2 a-strip) and the
dealt prints (r1 c-prints) in that new place. With the photo column gone, the exploded stack in About
gets the full width. References attached with -i: first the look the photos take in this variant
(`design/mockups/photos-r2-a-strip.png` for the strip, `design/mockups/photos-r1-c-prints.png` for the
prints), then the other one, for the page's layout, colours, type and the photos.

## Shared

A clean presentation board, 1536x1024, flat deep graphite #0A0B0C. On the left, about 1080 px wide, a
flat screenshot crop of one part of a desktop website (no browser chrome, no header). On the right,
one iPhone-style screen (390x844 proportions, thin dark bezel, rounded corners), straight on, no
perspective, no hands, showing the same part of the same website on a phone. Even gaps, nothing else
on the board, no watermark.

The crop shows the end of the About section at the top and the new photo section below it. In About
the exploded drawing of patent-style plates is now larger and wider, since nothing sits beside it on
desktop: the lowest two plates ("data" with fine contour rings and a lime dot, "infra" with a row of
slots, one in lime) are about 520 px wide, their leader lines running right to their labels in
monospace, with dashed vertical alignment lines between the plate corners. Under them "Download CV →"
with a lime underline. The thin lime vertical line runs down the far left of the page, with a small
lime waveform mark where the new section starts.

The new section's text, top left: the index "06 / OFF THE CLOCK" in small lime monospace; the headline
"Photography from Japan and Portugal." in the site's rounded grotesk, off-white; "All 67 photos →" in
off-white monospace with a thin lime underline.

The photos are real photographs: a temple roof, Mount Fuji, a lighthouse on a pier, an old tram, a red
torii gate in water. All grayscale except one in colour.

Palette: background deep graphite #0F1012 with a very subtle grain, text off-white #ECEAE4, dim text
#8A8C90, greys for line work, one accent acid lime #C8F542. No other hues except inside the one colour
photo, no glow, no gloss, no shadows, no 3D rendering. All text in English, crisp and legible.

Must not appear: photos beside the exploded drawing, cards, boxes or panels holding text, tables, tag
pills, icons, logos, emoji, camera illustrations, polaroids, tape, pins, handwriting, any person.

## a-strip

As in the first attached image: one long horizontal film strip runs across the full width of the page
under the section's text, cut off at both sides as if it keeps going. Fine off-white line work for the
film edges, small rectangular sprocket holes along both edges, tiny dim frame numbers (14, 15, 15A, 16,
17) along the edge. Frames about 300 px wide. The frame just right of centre sits inside a thin lime
bracket, is in colour and has a small dim monospace caption under it ("Portugal"). On the phone the
same strip runs across the full screen width under the section text, frames about 200 px wide, the
colour frame in the lime bracket in the middle.

## b-prints

As in the first attached image: the photos are five photographic prints with a thin off-white border,
dealt across the page under the section's text in one loose overlapping row that spans the content
width. Each print is slightly rotated (between -5 and 5 degrees), overlaps its neighbour by about a
fifth and sits a little higher or lower than the next, like prints spread on a table. Thin dashed
vertical lines, like the plates' alignment lines, run behind them. Each print has a small dim
monospace place under it ("Japan" or "Portugal"; the lighthouse and the tram are "Portugal"). The
middle print sits slightly forward, straight, and is in colour. On the phone three prints show in an
overlapping row across the full screen width, the middle one in colour, the fourth just peeking in at
the right edge.
