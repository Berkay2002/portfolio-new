# Photos r2: the photos as their own section

Round 1 (`photos-r1.md`) put the photos beside the exploded stack. Berkay liked the film strip in
a-filmstrip but found the stack and the strip side by side too much. This round takes the photos out of
About and gives them their own section on the landing page, "06 / OFF THE CLOCK", between About and
Contact (Contact becomes 07). About keeps only the headline, the stack and "Download CV". References
attached with -i: the round 1 film strip (`design/mockups/photos-r1-a-filmstrip.png`) first, for the
strip and the photos; then the current About section (`design/screens/about.1440.full.png`) for the
page's layout, colours and type.

## Shared

A clean presentation board, 1536x1024, flat deep graphite #0A0B0C. On the left, about 1080 px wide, a
flat screenshot crop of one part of a desktop website (no browser chrome, no header). On the right,
one iPhone-style screen (390x844 proportions, thin dark bezel, rounded corners), straight on, no
perspective, no hands, showing the same part of the same website on a phone. Even gaps, nothing else
on the board, no watermark.

The crop shows the bottom of the About section at the top ("Download CV →" with a lime underline and
the lower plate of the exploded drawing just above it, cut off by the crop edge), then the new photo
section below it with plenty of space around it. The thin lime vertical line runs down the far left of
the page as in the second attached image, with a small lime waveform mark where the new section starts.

The new section's text, top left, in the same layout as "05 / ABOUT": the index "06 / OFF THE CLOCK" in
small lime monospace; a short headline in the site's rounded grotesk, off-white, "Photography from
Japan and Portugal."; and "All 67 photos →" in off-white monospace with a thin lime underline.

The film strip is drawn as in the first attached image: fine off-white line work for the film edges,
small rectangular sprocket holes along both edges, tiny dim frame numbers (14, 15, 15A, 16, 17)
printed along the edge like real film. The frames are real photographs: a temple roof, Mount Fuji, a
lighthouse on a pier, an old tram, a red torii gate, a pagoda. All grayscale except the one frame inside
a thin lime bracket, which is in colour, with a small dim monospace caption under it ("Portugal").

Palette: background deep graphite #0F1012 with a very subtle grain, text off-white #ECEAE4, dim text
#8A8C90, greys for line work, one accent acid lime #C8F542. No other hues except inside the one colour
photo, no glow, no gloss, no shadows, no 3D rendering. All text in English, crisp and legible.

Must not appear: photos beside the exploded drawing, cards, boxes or panels holding text, tables, tag
pills, icons, logos, emoji, camera illustrations, polaroids, tape, pins, any person.

## a-strip

One long horizontal film strip runs across the full width of the page, edge to edge, cut off at both
sides as if it keeps going, under the section's text. Frames about 300 px wide on desktop, the lime
bracket around the frame just right of centre. On the phone the section text sits above and the same
strip runs across the full screen width, frames about 200 px wide, cut off at both screen edges, the
colour frame in the lime bracket in the middle.

## b-contact

The photos are a contact sheet: three short film strips laid one under another, each holding five
frames, slightly offset left and right as if laid by hand, filling the content width beside and under
the section's text. One frame on the middle strip is circled with a thin hand-drawn lime grease-pencil
rectangle instead of the bracket and is in colour, with its caption under it. On the phone, two strips
of three frames each, one under the other, full width, the marked frame in colour.
